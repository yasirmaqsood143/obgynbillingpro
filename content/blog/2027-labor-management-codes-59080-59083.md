For the first time, CPT will have dedicated codes for **managing labor**. Under the global maternity package, labor management was simply part of the delivery fee — whether labor lasted four hours or three days. Starting **January 1, 2027**, the CPT maternity section separates labor from delivery and reports it with four new per-day codes.

This article explains the new codes based on the AMA's published guidance, and what OB/GYN practices should do to prepare. Because the official CPT 2027 codebook language is the final word, treat any summary — including this one — as a starting point and confirm the details when your codebook arrives.

## The four new codes

| Code | Descriptor (summary) |
|---|---|
| `59080` | Initial day of labor management, straightforward |
| `59081` | Initial day of labor management, complex |
| `59082` | Subsequent day of labor management, straightforward |
| `59083` | Subsequent day of labor management, complex |

The codes are reported **per day** — a calendar date — so a labor that starts on Monday evening and ends with a delivery on Tuesday can involve an initial-day code on Monday and a subsequent-day code on Tuesday.

## Straightforward vs. complex

The AMA's guidance sets out criteria for straightforward labor and states that **labor is complex if any straightforward criterion isn't met**. Two other rules from the AMA FAQ are worth memorizing:

- **Multiple gestations are complex by default.**
- **Only one complex code is reported per calendar date**, regardless of the number of fetuses.

Published summaries of the new guidelines describe straightforward labor along the lines of a singleton, vertex presentation with routine monitoring, normal progress (or a routine induction), stable maternal and fetal status, and no previous cesarean. Factors that make labor complex in those summaries include a prior cesarean (trial of labor after cesarean), multiple gestation, malpresentation, prolonged labor stages and labor complications. Because the precise criteria matter for every claim, build your documentation templates from the official CPT 2027 language once it's available.

## When labor management doesn't apply

- **Planned cesarean.** The AMA states there's no labor management code for a planned cesarean delivery. The cesarean itself is reported with the new cesarean delivery code.
- **Days with no labor.** Antepartum hospital care before labor begins — for example, observation for preeclampsia without labor — is reported with hospital E/M codes, not labor management.

## How labor management interacts with other services

### Hospital E/M on the same day

According to the AMA's guidance, hospital E/M services by the same clinician **stop once labor management begins that day**. In practice:

- If the patient was admitted for another reason earlier in the day and seen by the clinician, then went into labor, the clinician's reporting shifts to labor management once labor begins.
- You shouldn't bill both an inpatient E/M and a labor management code for the same clinician's work on the same date once labor management has started.

### Induction and augmentation

Published summaries indicate that interventions routinely part of labor — such as induction, augmentation and amniotomy — are included in labor management rather than billed separately. Confirm against the final CPT guidance and payer policy, particularly for cervical ripening procedures that have their own codes today.

### Delivery

Labor management and delivery are reported separately under the new structure. A vaginal birth on the day labor ends would typically include a labor management code for that date plus the vaginal delivery code (`59431`, or `59432` for a vaginal birth after cesarean). See our guide to the [2027 delivery codes](/blog/2027-delivery-codes-59431-59502).

### Group practice and multiple clinicians

The new framework is designed to reflect team-based obstetric care. When more than one clinician in the same group manages a patient's labor on the same date, expect rules similar to other per-day services: the group reports one labor management code for that date. Payer policies on rendering provider assignment will matter — watch for them.

## Worked examples

**Example 1 — Straightforward labor and same-day delivery.** A healthy patient with a singleton, vertex pregnancy at term is admitted in spontaneous labor at 6 a.m. and delivers vaginally at 4 p.m.

- `59080` — initial day of labor management, straightforward
- `59431` — vaginal delivery

**Example 2 — Labor over two days, then cesarean.** A patient is admitted in labor at 9 p.m. Labor continues overnight; arrest of dilation leads to a primary cesarean at 2 p.m. the next day. Labor met the straightforward criteria on day one, but prolonged labor made it complex on day two.

- Day 1: `59080` — initial day, straightforward
- Day 2: `59083` — subsequent day, complex
- Day 2: `59502` — primary cesarean delivery

**Example 3 — Twins.** A patient with dichorionic/diamniotic twins labors and delivers both vaginally on the same day.

- `59081` — initial day, complex (multiples are complex by default; only one code per date)
- Delivery codes per the CPT 2027 delivery guidelines for multiple births

**Example 4 — Planned repeat cesarean.** A scheduled repeat cesarean at 39 weeks without labor.

- No labor management code
- `59503` — repeat cesarean delivery

These examples are illustrative; confirm the reporting of multiple-birth deliveries and day-boundary rules in the final CPT 2027 guidelines.

## Documentation that will support labor management codes

Because complexity depends on specific criteria, documentation needs to show them clearly for each date:

- **Time labor began** and the clinician's first labor-management encounter that day
- **Presentation and number of fetuses**
- **History of previous cesarean** (and whether this is a trial of labor)
- **Labor progress**, including any diagnosis of prolonged or arrested labor
- **Monitoring findings** and any interventions required
- **Maternal and fetal status**, including complications
- **Which clinician** managed labor on each date

Expect payers to scrutinize complex codes, especially when the complexity rests on a single factor. Clear notes for each calendar date will be essential.

## Why CPT separated labor from delivery

Under the global package, a 4-hour labor and a 40-hour labor were paid the same, and a straightforward labor was paid the same as one that required intensive management of a trial of labor after cesarean. The restructuring aims to better reflect the actual work involved and the team-based way obstetric care is delivered — where the clinician managing labor overnight may not be the one who delivers the baby. Per-day codes with a complexity distinction are the mechanism for that.

For practices, that means labor management becomes a visible, documentable service rather than an invisible part of a package. It also means payers will see, for the first time, how often a practice reports complex labor. Practices should expect payers to watch the ratio of complex to straightforward codes, the same way they watch E/M level distributions today. Accurate, criteria-based documentation is the best protection.

## Questions to settle inside your group before January

- **Who reports labor management** when a laborist, hospitalist or covering physician manages part of labor?
- **How will labor documentation reach your billing team** — directly from the hospital EHR, through a delivery summary, or a daily charge sheet?
- **How will day boundaries be handled** for labors that cross midnight?
- **Who reviews complexity** before claims are submitted, especially for single-factor complex claims?
- **How will midwives and physicians share** labor management within the group, and which rendering provider appears on the claim?

Write the answers into a short internal policy so every clinician and biller applies them the same way.
