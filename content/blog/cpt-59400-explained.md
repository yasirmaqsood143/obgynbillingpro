`59400` is the code most obstetric practices know by heart — and the single most valuable code on many practices' fee schedules. It's also the code most often billed when it shouldn't be, and *not* billed when it should. This guide covers exactly what `59400` includes, when to use something else, how and when to bill it, and what happens to it on January 1, 2027.

## CPT 59400 description

**CPT 59400: Routine obstetric care including antepartum care, vaginal delivery (with or without episiotomy, and/or forceps) and postpartum care.**

It's a **global** (package) code: one code, one claim, one payment covering the whole course of routine maternity care for a vaginal delivery by one physician or group.

## What's included in 59400

Per the CPT maternity care guidelines, the package includes:

**Antepartum care**
- Initial and subsequent history and physical examinations
- Recording of weight, blood pressure and fetal heart tones
- Routine chemical urinalysis
- Routine visits — typically monthly up to 28 weeks, biweekly to 36 weeks, and weekly until delivery

**Delivery**
- Admission to the hospital and the admission history and physical
- Management of uncomplicated labor
- Vaginal delivery, with or without episiotomy and/or forceps
- Delivery of the placenta

**Postpartum care**
- Hospital visits after delivery
- Routine office visit(s) following vaginal delivery

## What's NOT included in 59400

Services outside routine care are billed separately — and missing them is one of the most common sources of lost obstetric revenue:

| Service | How it's billed |
|---|---|
| Obstetric ultrasounds | `76801`–`76828` as performed |
| Fetal non-stress test | `59025` |
| Amniocentesis | `59000` |
| External cephalic version | `59412` |
| Laboratory tests | Lab codes (if performed in-office) |
| Visits for complications or unrelated conditions | E/M codes with the appropriate O-code diagnosis, per payer policy |
| Cervical cerclage | `59320` / `59325` |
| Postpartum procedures beyond routine care | Procedure codes as applicable |

Whether a complication visit is separately billable depends on the payer. Many follow the principle that problems beyond routine care are separately reportable when documented with diagnoses that show the complication; some Medicaid programs have their own rules.

## When NOT to use 59400

`59400` only fits when **the same physician or group** provided **all three** components and the baby was delivered **vaginally** **without a previous cesarean**. Use something else when:

| Situation | Use instead |
|---|---|
| Previous cesarean, vaginal birth now (VBAC) | `59610` |
| Cesarean delivery | `59510` (or `59618` after an attempted VBAC) |
| Your practice provided only some antepartum visits | Component codes — E/M (1–3 visits), `59425` (4–6) or `59426` (7+) |
| You delivered but didn't provide antepartum care | `59409` (delivery only) or `59410` (delivery + postpartum) |
| You provided only postpartum care | `59430` |
| Insurance changed mid-pregnancy | Split billing by coverage period |

See our detailed guides on [59409 vs 59410](/blog/cpt-59409-vs-59410-delivery-codes) and [split maternity care billing](/blog/split-maternity-care-billing-transfer-example).

## When and how to bill 59400

- **Date of service:** most payers expect the **delivery date**. Some want a date span from the first antepartum visit to delivery or the postpartum visit. Follow the payer's manual.
- **Timing:** many practices bill after delivery; some wait for the postpartum visit. Waiting too long risks commercial timely-filing limits, so know each payer's deadline.
- **Notification:** some Medicaid programs and plans require a pregnancy notification or first-visit claim (sometimes with modifier `TH`) early in the pregnancy. See our guide to the [TH modifier](/blog/th-modifier-obstetric-billing).
- **Track visits throughout:** keep a running count of antepartum visits and payer at each visit. If the pregnancy doesn't end with your practice delivering, you'll need that count to bill the right component code.

## Diagnosis codes on a 59400 claim

- **Principal diagnosis:** the main circumstance or complication of the delivery. For a full-term, uncomplicated delivery of a single liveborn infant with no complications, `O80` is used — alone, with no other Chapter 15 codes.
- **`Z3A.__`** — weeks of gestation at delivery.
- **`Z37.__`** — outcome of delivery, required on every delivery claim (for example `Z37.0`, single live birth).
- Relevant conditions that complicated the pregnancy or delivery — see our [ICD-10 O codes guide](/resources/icd10-o-codes).

## Twins and 59400

For a vaginal delivery of twins, many payers expect `59400` for the first baby and `59409` with modifier `51` or `59` for the second. Others want modifier `22` on a single code. Policies differ, so check each payer.

## Modifier 22 on 59400

Modifier `22` (increased procedural services) may be appropriate for deliveries requiring substantially more work than usual — but it must be supported by documentation describing the additional work. Payers frequently reduce or deny `22` without it.

## Common 59400 errors

1. **Billing `59400` for a VBAC** — should be `59610`.
2. **Billing `59400` after a transfer-in** — the practice didn't provide all antepartum care.
3. **Billing the global to the plan active at delivery** when another plan covered most of the pregnancy.
4. **Separately billing routine prenatal visits** that are already in the global — producing `CO-97` denials. See our guide to the [CO-97 denial code](/blog/co-97-denial-code-explained).
5. **Missing separately billable services** — ultrasounds, NSTs, complication visits.
6. **Missing `Z37`** or using `O80` with other O codes.
7. **Holding the claim too long** after delivery.

## 59400 and global period tracking

Medicare's Physician Fee Schedule lists maternity codes with the global indicator `MMM`, meaning the standard surgical global days don't apply; the maternity guidelines define what's included. Internally, practices should still track each pregnancy's "global window" — from the first antepartum visit through the postpartum visit — to prevent routine visits from being billed separately and to catch transfers and insurance changes.

## The end of 59400: January 1, 2027

The AMA's CPT 2027 code set **deletes `59400`** (along with the rest of the global and component maternity codes) for dates of service on or after January 1, 2027. A routine vaginal pregnancy will instead be reported as:

- Prenatal visits → E/M codes, per encounter
- Labor → `59080`–`59083`, per day
- Vaginal delivery → `59431` (or `59432` after a previous cesarean)
- Postpartum care → included on the delivery date; later hospital days and office visits reported with E/M codes

Patients whose pregnancies span the change will need careful handling. Read our [2027 maternity transition guide](/resources/2027-maternity-transition) and our explainer on the [2027 delivery codes](/blog/2027-delivery-codes-59431-59502).
