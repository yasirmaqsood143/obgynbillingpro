Most vaginal deliveries in a practice that provided the patient's prenatal care are billed with the global code `59400`. But a meaningful share of deliveries don't fit the global package — the patient transferred in late, changed insurance, or was delivered by a physician covering for another practice. That's where the delivery-only codes come in, and where `59409` and `59410` are most often confused.

## The descriptors

| Code | Descriptor (summary) | Includes |
|---|---|---|
| `59409` | Vaginal delivery only (with or without episiotomy and/or forceps) | Admission history and physical, management of labor, vaginal delivery, and routine care immediately related to the delivery |
| `59410` | Vaginal delivery only (with or without episiotomy and/or forceps); **including postpartum care** | Everything in `59409` plus routine postpartum hospital care and the postpartum office visit(s) |
| `59400` | Routine obstetric care including antepartum care, vaginal delivery and postpartum care | Antepartum care + delivery + postpartum |
| `59430` | Postpartum care only (separate procedure) | Postpartum care when the delivery was billed by someone else |

The difference between `59409` and `59410` is simply **who provides the postpartum care**. If your practice delivered the baby and will see the patient for routine postpartum care, bill `59410`. If someone outside your practice will provide postpartum care, bill `59409`.

## The global period for 59410

Searches for "59410 global period" are common, and the answer surprises people. In the Medicare Physician Fee Schedule, maternity codes such as `59400`, `59409` and `59410` carry a global surgery indicator of **`MMM`**. That means the standard 0-, 10- and 90-day surgical global rules don't apply. Instead, what's included is defined by the CPT maternity care guidelines:

- **Delivery services** include admission to the hospital, the admission history and physical, management of uncomplicated labor, and the vaginal delivery (with or without episiotomy and/or forceps).
- **Postpartum care** (in `59410`) includes hospital visits after delivery and routine office visits following vaginal delivery — commonly around six weeks.

Problems unrelated to routine postpartum care — for example, treating a postpartum infection or depression beyond routine screening — may be separately billable with an E/M code and the appropriate diagnosis.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: Patient transferred in at 34 weeks

The patient received prenatal care elsewhere until 34 weeks, then transferred to your practice. You provide four more antepartum visits, deliver vaginally, and see her for postpartum care.

- `59425` — antepartum care only, 4–6 visits (the four visits your practice provided)
- `59410` — vaginal delivery including postpartum care

The prior practice bills its own antepartum care. For a full split-care walkthrough, read [split maternity care billing](/blog/split-maternity-care-billing-transfer-example).

### Example 2: Covering physician from another group

Dr. A (a different group) is on call and delivers a patient of Dr. B's practice. Dr. B's practice provided all prenatal care and will provide postpartum care.

- Dr. A bills `59409` — delivery only
- Dr. B's practice bills `59425` or `59426` for antepartum care, depending on visit count, and `59430` for postpartum care

If Dr. A and Dr. B are in the **same group**, the group generally bills the global `59400` instead.

### Example 3: Insurance changed before delivery

Your practice provided all care, but the patient switched insurance at 30 weeks. Antepartum care before the switch goes to the first plan (`59425`/`59426` or E/M visits); the remaining antepartum care plus delivery and postpartum go to the new plan — often as additional antepartum components and `59410`, depending on the payer's rules.

### Example 4: The patient had a previous cesarean

If the vaginal delivery follows a previous cesarean, it's a VBAC and the VBAC codes apply: `59612` (delivery only) or `59614` (delivery including postpartum care) — not `59409`/`59410`.

## Diagnosis coding for delivery claims

Every delivery claim needs a complete diagnosis set:

- **Principal diagnosis** — the main circumstance or complication of the delivery, or `O80` for a full-term, uncomplicated delivery of a single liveborn infant with no complications (used alone with `Z37.0`)
- **`Z3A.__`** — weeks of gestation
- **`Z37.__`** — outcome of delivery (for example `Z37.0`, single live birth) — required on every maternal delivery record

For complications, sequence the condition responsible for the main circumstance first. See our guide to [sequencing multiple O codes](/blog/multiple-o-codes-sequencing-pregnancy).

## Additional services during the delivery admission

Some services alongside a vaginal delivery are reported separately, depending on payer policy:

- **Third- or fourth-degree laceration repair** — under current rules, repair is typically part of the delivery; when the repair is performed by someone other than the delivering physician, `59300` applies. (Starting in 2027, new codes `59433` and `59434` report third- and fourth-degree repairs with the delivery code.)
- **Delivery of placenta separately** — `59414`, when the delivery of the placenta is performed by someone other than the person who delivered the baby
- **External cephalic version** — `59412`
- **Postpartum curettage** — `59160`
- **Immediate postpartum LARC** — insertion code plus device, though many hospitals bill the device on the facility claim; confirm who supplies and bills the device

## Twins and multiple gestations

Payers handle twin vaginal deliveries differently. A common approach is the appropriate global or delivery code for the first baby and `59409` with a multiple-procedure (`51`) or distinct-procedure (`59`) modifier for the second. Some payers instead want modifier `22` on a single delivery code. Follow each payer's published policy.

## Assistant at delivery

Vaginal delivery codes often don't allow an assistant surgeon for Medicare. Check each payer's assistant-at-surgery indicator before billing `80`, `82` or `AS` on `59409`/`59410`.

## Common 59409/59410 billing errors

1. Billing `59410` when another group provides postpartum care (or `59409` when you do)
2. Billing `59400` when the practice didn't provide antepartum care
3. Missing the antepartum component claim for transfers in
4. Using `59409`/`59410` for a VBAC (should be `59612`/`59614`)
5. Billing a separate E/M for routine postpartum visits included in `59410`
6. Missing `Z37` or `Z3A` on the claim

## What changes on January 1, 2027

Under the CPT 2027 maternity care changes, `59400`, `59409`, `59410` and `59430` are deleted for dates of service on or after January 1, 2027. Vaginal deliveries will be reported with **`59431`** (or **`59432`** after a previous cesarean), labor management with new codes `59080`–`59083`, and postpartum visits with E/M codes. Routine same-day postpartum care will be included in the delivery code. Read our guide to the [2027 delivery codes](/blog/2027-delivery-codes-59431-59502) and the full [2027 maternity transition guide](/resources/2027-maternity-transition).

## How payers process 59409 and 59410

Payers generally expect delivery-only and delivery-plus-postpartum codes to appear **with** — not instead of — documentation of who provided the rest of the care. A few practical points:

- **Expect edits against other providers' claims.** If another practice has already billed a global code for the same pregnancy, your delivery-only claim may be denied as a duplicate. The fix is usually coordination between practices, not resubmission.
- **Date of service conventions vary.** Most payers expect the delivery date as the date of service for `59409`/`59410`. Some Medicaid programs specify a date span. Follow the payer's manual.
- **Postpartum care must actually be provided.** If you bill `59410` and the patient never returns for postpartum care, some payers expect the code to be changed to `59409` — and a postpartum visit that occurs with another practice will be billed by them as `59430`.
- **Hospital visits after delivery** are included in `59410`'s routine postpartum care. Separately billed hospital E/M visits on postpartum days are likely to deny unless they address a complication with its own documented diagnosis.

## Quick decision guide

1. Did your practice (or group) provide all routine antepartum care? **Yes** → consider the global code `59400`. **No** → continue.
2. Did your practice deliver the baby vaginally? **Yes** → continue.
3. Was there a previous cesarean? **Yes** → use VBAC codes `59612`/`59614`. **No** → continue.
4. Will your practice provide the routine postpartum care? **Yes** → `59410`. **No** → `59409`.
5. Did your practice provide antepartum visits? Bill them separately — E/M for 1–3 visits, `59425` for 4–6, `59426` for 7 or more.
