Endometriosis surgery ranges from a quick diagnostic laparoscopy with ablation of a few superficial implants to a multi-hour excision of deep infiltrating disease involving the bowel, bladder and ureters. CPT doesn't distinguish nearly as finely as the clinical reality does, which puts the burden on documentation and on careful decisions about bundling, modifiers and co-surgery.

## The primary code: 58662

**`58662`: Laparoscopy, surgical; with fulguration or excision of lesions of the ovary, pelvic viscera, or peritoneal surface by any method.**

Key points:

- **"By any method"** — excision, ablation, fulguration, laser vaporization all use `58662`.
- **Robotic-assisted** excision uses `58662`; there's no robotic-specific code.
- **Not bilateral** — `58662` carries a bilateral surgery indicator of 0. The descriptor covers lesions anywhere on the pelvic viscera or peritoneum, so it's reported once.
- **Diagnostic laparoscopy** (`49320`) is included and never reported separately.

## When endometriosis surgery accompanies other procedures

### With hysterectomy

NCCI procedure-to-procedure edits bundle `58662` into many laparoscopic hysterectomy codes (for example the `58570`–`58573` family). The rationale: excising lesions in the operative field is part of performing the hysterectomy.

Separate reporting with modifier `59` or `XS` may be supportable when the documentation shows endometriosis excision at **distinct anatomic sites** outside the field of the hysterectomy and its adnexal work — for example, extensive excision from the pelvic sidewalls, uterosacral ligaments and posterior cul-de-sac requiring substantial additional dissection. The operative note must describe each site. When in doubt, modifier `22` on the hysterectomy, with documentation of the additional work and time, is often the more defensible approach.

### With ovarian cystectomy (endometrioma)

Excision of an endometrioma with preservation of the ovary is commonly reported with `58662` itself. If an oophorectomy is performed on one side and peritoneal disease excised elsewhere, review NCCI edits for `58661` and `58662` and documentation of distinct sites.

### With adhesiolysis

`58660` (laparoscopic lysis of adhesions) is generally bundled with `58662`. Extensive adhesiolysis is better reflected through modifier `22`.

### With hysteroscopy or other procedures in a different approach

Hysteroscopic procedures performed at the same session (for example `58558`) are a different approach and are often separately reportable, subject to edits.

## Deep infiltrating endometriosis and multi-specialty cases

When disease involves the bowel, bladder or ureters, other procedures — and often other surgeons — become involved:

| Organ | Examples of separately reported procedures |
|---|---|
| Bowel | Laparoscopic colectomy or rectal resection codes; disc excision or shaving reported per colorectal coding guidance |
| Bladder | Partial cystectomy codes |
| Ureter | Ureterolysis (for example `50715`, open; laparoscopic approaches vary), ureteral reimplantation, stent placement (`52332`) |
| Appendix | Appendectomy for documented disease |

When a gynecologic surgeon and a colorectal or urologic surgeon each perform distinct parts of a single procedure, modifier `62` (co-surgeons) may apply; when each performs separate procedures, each bills their own codes. Coordinate before the case so both practices bill consistently. Ureterolysis performed as a routine part of a gynecologic procedure is often bundled — separate reporting requires documented medical necessity beyond the usual dissection.

## Modifier 22 for complex endometriosis

Modifier `22` (increased procedural services) is the main tool for recognizing extensive endometriosis excision that CPT codes don't fully describe. To support it:

- Describe the **extent** and **locations** of disease and why it made the procedure substantially more difficult
- Document **operative time** compared with a typical case
- Note any **additional risk** (proximity to ureters, bowel, vessels)
- Submit the operative note with the claim, or be ready to on request

Payers often reduce or deny `22` without documentation. With it, many will increase payment.

## ICD-10-CM endometriosis codes (N80)

The `N80` category was expanded in recent years to specify site, laterality and depth. Highlights:

| Code range | Site |
|---|---|
| `N80.0-` | Endometriosis of uterus, including `N80.03` adenomyosis |
| `N80.1-` | Endometriosis of ovary (superficial, deep, by laterality) |
| `N80.2-` | Endometriosis of fallopian tube |
| `N80.3-` | Endometriosis of pelvic peritoneum (by location — cul-de-sac, broad ligament, uterosacral ligaments, pelvic sidewall and others) |
| `N80.4-` | Endometriosis of rectovaginal septum and vagina |
| `N80.5-` | Endometriosis of intestine (by segment and depth) |
| `N80.6` | Endometriosis in cutaneous scar |
| `N80.A-` | Endometriosis of bladder |
| `N80.B-` | Endometriosis of cardiothoracic space |
| `N80.C-` | Endometriosis of abdomen |
| `N80.D-` | Endometriosis of pelvic nerves |
| `N80.9` | Endometriosis, unspecified |

Code every documented site to its highest specificity. Detailed diagnosis codes support medical necessity for extensive surgery, modifier `22`, and co-surgery — and they reflect the complexity that CPT doesn't capture.

## Prior authorization

Many payers require authorization for laparoscopic endometriosis surgery, especially when hysterectomy or bowel procedures are planned. Request authorization for all procedures you reasonably expect to perform, and update it promptly if the plan changes intraoperatively. See [eligibility verification](/eligibility-verification).

## Documentation checklist

- Indication, symptoms and prior treatments
- Every site of disease, with laterality, size and depth (superficial vs. deep)
- Method used at each site (excision, ablation)
- Organs involved and which surgeon performed which part
- Adhesions and their impact on the procedure
- Operative time and any unusual difficulty
- Pathology confirmation

## Common errors

1. Reporting `49320` diagnostic laparoscopy with `58662`
2. Reporting `58662-50` (it isn't bilateral)
3. Appending `59` to `58662` with a hysterectomy without documenting distinct sites
4. Using `N80.9` when the op note describes specific sites
5. Missing co-surgeon coordination on bowel cases
6. Billing ureterolysis routinely when it was part of standard dissection
