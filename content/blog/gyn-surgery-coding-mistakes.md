Gynecologic surgery claims are among the highest-value claims an OB/GYN practice submits — which means small coding errors have large consequences. After years of reviewing surgical claims, we see the same handful of mistakes again and again. Here they are, with the fix for each.

## 1. Coding the wrong surgical approach

The same procedure has different codes for abdominal, vaginal, laparoscopic and laparoscopically assisted vaginal approaches — with very different values.

**Common errors:**
- Coding a laparoscopically assisted vaginal hysterectomy (`58550`–`58554`) as a total laparoscopic hysterectomy (`58570`–`58573`), or vice versa
- Coding a robotic myomectomy with open codes (`58140`/`58146`) instead of laparoscopic (`58545`/`58546`)
- Missing a **conversion** from laparoscopic to open, which changes the code to the open procedure

**Fix:** code from the procedure description in the operative note, not the scheduled procedure name. For robotic cases, see [CPT code for robotic hysterectomy](/blog/cpt-code-robotic-hysterectomy).

## 2. Billing before pathology

Laparoscopic and vaginal hysterectomy codes split at **250 grams**; myomectomy codes split by **number and total weight** of intramural fibroids. Billing before the pathology report means guessing.

**Fix:** hold these claims until pathology is final, and keep a short weekly list of surgical claims waiting on reports so they don't stall.

## 3. Missing adnexal procedures

Hysterectomy codes "with removal of tube(s) and/or ovary(s)" apply when **any** tube or ovary is removed — including opportunistic bilateral salpingectomy with ovarian preservation.

**Fix:** read the whole operative note for every adnexal structure removed. `58570` with a bilateral salpingectomy should be `58571`.

## 4. Unbundling components that should be bundled

NCCI edits bundle many procedures commonly performed together:

- Diagnostic laparoscopy (`49320`) into surgical laparoscopy
- Diagnostic hysteroscopy (`58555`) into surgical hysteroscopy
- Lysis of adhesions (`58660`) into most laparoscopic procedures
- Endometriosis excision (`58662`) into hysterectomy in the same field
- Cystoscopy into hysterectomy or pelvic floor repair, when performed to check integrity
- Laparoscopic adnexal removal (`58661`) into hysterectomy codes that already include it

**Fix:** NCCI edits in your claim scrubber, and a rule that bypass modifiers require documentation. See our [modifier 59 and X-modifiers guide](/blog/modifier-59-xs-gyn-surgery-ncci).

## 5. Misusing modifier 59

Appending `59` to every bundled pair turns an edit into an audit target. Modifier `59` (or `XE`, `XS`, `XP`, `XU`) is only for **distinct** services — a separate session, structure, practitioner or non-overlapping service — documented as such.

**Fix:** check the NCCI modifier indicator and the operative note before every bypass. If the work was extra but not distinct, consider modifier `22` instead.

## 6. Missing modifier 22 when it's justified

The opposite error: complex cases billed like routine ones. Dense adhesions, extensive endometriosis, a very large uterus beyond the weight-tier distinction, or obesity that significantly increases operative time can all justify modifier `22`.

**Fix:** educate surgeons to document the nature of the difficulty and the extra time; submit the operative note with the claim. Without documentation, payers usually ignore `22`.

## 7. Wrong use of modifier 50

Some GYN codes allow bilateral billing and some don't. For example, `58661` has a Medicare bilateral indicator that allows modifier `50` when both sides are treated, while `58662` and codes whose descriptors say "unilateral or bilateral" (such as `58700`, `58720`, `58940`) don't take it. Hysterectomy codes that include adnexa never do.

**Fix:** check the bilateral surgery indicator and payer policy for each code. See our [adnexal surgery coding guide](/blog/laparoscopic-oophorectomy-ovarian-cystectomy-cpt).

## 8. Global period mistakes

Most major GYN surgeries have a 90-day global period.

**Common errors:**
- Billing routine post-op visits (should be `99024`, no charge)
- Missing modifier `24` on a visit for an unrelated problem during the global period
- Missing `78` for a return to the OR for a complication, `79` for an unrelated procedure, or `58` for a staged procedure

**Fix:** global period flags in the PM system and a quick global-period check before every claim.

## 9. Diagnosis codes that don't support the procedure

A hysterectomy coded only with pelvic pain, or a diagnosis that contradicts pathology, invites medical necessity denials.

**Fix:** code to the pathology and the documented indication — fibroids by location (`D25.-`), endometriosis by site (`N80.-`), prolapse (`N81.-`), abnormal bleeding (`N92.-`/`N93.-`). See our [endometriosis coding guide](/blog/endometriosis-excision-cpt-coding) and [myomectomy CPT codes](/blog/myomectomy-cpt-codes).

## 10. Authorization mismatches

Surgery authorized as one CPT code and billed as another — after a weight tier or adnexal change — can deny even though the surgery was approved.

**Fix:** authorize for the codes you reasonably expect (including higher weight tiers when imaging suggests them) and update authorizations promptly when the procedure changes.

## A quick pre-submission checklist for surgical claims

- [ ] Approach matches the operative note (including conversions)
- [ ] Pathology received; weight tier and diagnosis confirmed
- [ ] All adnexal procedures captured
- [ ] NCCI edits checked; modifiers supported by documentation
- [ ] Modifier `22` considered and documented where appropriate
- [ ] Bilateral rules checked
- [ ] Global period status checked
- [ ] Authorization matches billed codes
- [ ] Assistant surgeon eligibility checked
