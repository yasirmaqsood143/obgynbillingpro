Long-acting reversible contraception (LARC) — intrauterine devices and contraceptive implants — is a routine part of OB/GYN practice. The procedures are quick. The billing isn't always: each LARC encounter can involve a procedure code, a device code, a possible same-day E/M, a preventive-services modifier and payer-specific supply rules. Miss the device code and the practice may absorb the cost of a device it purchased.

## LARC procedure codes

| Code | Descriptor (summary) |
|---|---|
| `58300` | Insertion of intrauterine device (IUD) |
| `58301` | Removal of intrauterine device (IUD) |
| `11981` | Insertion, non-biodegradable drug delivery implant |
| `11982` | Removal, non-biodegradable drug delivery implant |
| `11983` | Removal with reinsertion, non-biodegradable drug delivery implant |

For implants, `11983` is used when one implant is removed and another inserted at the same visit — don't report `11982` and `11981` together.

For IUDs, removal and reinsertion at the same visit is usually reported with `58301` and `58300`; some payers ask for a modifier on the second code. Follow payer policy.

## Device codes

| HCPCS | Device |
|---|---|
| `J7296` | Levonorgestrel-releasing intrauterine contraceptive system, 19.5 mg (Kyleena) |
| `J7297` | Levonorgestrel-releasing intrauterine contraceptive system, 52 mg (Liletta) |
| `J7298` | Levonorgestrel-releasing intrauterine contraceptive system, 52 mg (Mirena) |
| `J7300` | Intrauterine copper contraceptive (Paragard) |
| `J7301` | Levonorgestrel-releasing intrauterine contraceptive system, 13.5 mg (Skyla) |
| `J7307` | Etonogestrel (contraceptive) implant system, including implant and supplies (Nexplanon) |

Report the device code **only when your practice purchased the device**. If the patient's pharmacy benefit supplied it (often through a specialty pharmacy), the pharmacy bills the device and you bill only the insertion. Some Medicaid programs and 340B arrangements have their own device-billing rules — and some require NDC information on the claim.

## The Affordable Care Act and LARC

Under the ACA's preventive services requirements, most non-grandfathered health plans must cover FDA-approved contraceptive methods — including LARC and the related insertion and removal services — without patient cost-sharing, subject to plan rules such as reasonable medical management. In practice:

- Many payers ask for **modifier `33`** (preventive service) or a contraception diagnosis to waive cost-sharing.
- Diagnosis codes from **`Z30.-`** (encounter for contraceptive management) are used — for example `Z30.430` (insertion of IUD), `Z30.432` (removal of IUD), `Z30.433` (removal and reinsertion of IUD), `Z30.017` (initial prescription of implantable subdermal contraceptive), `Z30.46` (surveillance of implantable subdermal contraceptive).

Exact codes depend on the encounter. Use the most specific `Z30` code that matches the documentation.

## Same-day E/M visits and modifier 25

Every procedure includes the usual pre-procedure evaluation — confirming the patient is a candidate, obtaining consent, reviewing contraindications. That work isn't separately billable.

A separate E/M visit **with modifier `25`** is appropriate when the provider performs significant, separately identifiable work, such as:

- Evaluating and managing an unrelated problem (abnormal bleeding, pelvic pain, vaginitis)
- A preventive visit at which the LARC is also inserted (bill the preventive visit with modifier `25` where the payer requires it)
- Extensive contraceptive counseling for a patient who ultimately decides against LARC at that visit — in which case only the E/M is billed

When counseling leads to a decision and the insertion happens at the same visit, many payers consider routine counseling part of the procedure. Document the separate problem or work clearly if you bill the E/M.

## Pregnancy testing and related services

Point-of-care urine pregnancy tests (`81025`) performed before insertion are commonly billable if the practice holds the appropriate CLIA certificate. Ultrasound to confirm IUD placement, when medically necessary, is separately reportable with the appropriate pelvic ultrasound code. Routine follow-up "string checks" are typically billed with an E/M code only if they meet E/M requirements.

## Postpartum LARC

Immediate postpartum LARC — placed after delivery, before hospital discharge — has its own considerations:

- **Who supplies the device?** Often the hospital, which bills the device on the facility claim. Some state Medicaid programs specifically allow the device to be billed outside the inpatient DRG; others pay the practitioner for the device. Confirm the arrangement before billing.
- **Is the insertion separately payable?** Many payers pay the insertion separately from the global obstetric fee; some don't. Check the payer's maternity and contraception policies.
- **Diagnosis codes:** use the appropriate `Z30` code along with any required postpartum codes.

## IUD complications and laparoscopic removal

A migrated or embedded IUD that requires hysteroscopic removal may be reported with `58562` (hysteroscopy with removal of impacted foreign body). Laparoscopic retrieval of an IUD that has perforated into the abdomen has no specific CPT code and is usually reported with the appropriate **unlisted laparoscopy code**, with the operative note, a comparison code and an explanation. Complications are coded with `T83.3-` codes (mechanical complications of intrauterine contraceptive device), with the appropriate seventh character.

## Inventory and charge capture

Because LARC devices are expensive, inventory control is part of billing:

- **Log each device** received, its lot number and which patient it was used for.
- **Match the device on the claim to the device in the log** — billing `J7298` for a `J7297` device is a billing error.
- **Reconcile monthly:** devices used vs. devices billed vs. devices paid.
- **Track pharmacy-supplied devices** separately so you never bill a device you didn't buy.

## Common LARC billing errors

1. Insertion billed without the device code (when the practice bought the device)
2. Device billed when the pharmacy supplied it
3. Wrong J-code for the brand inserted
4. `11982` + `11981` billed instead of `11983`
5. E/M with modifier `25` billed for routine pre-procedure work
6. Missing modifier `33` or `Z30` diagnosis, causing patient cost-sharing errors
7. Postpartum LARC device billed by both the practice and the hospital
