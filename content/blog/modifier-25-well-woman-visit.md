A patient arrives for her annual well-woman exam and mentions heavier periods over the past six months. Her physician evaluates the bleeding, orders labs and an ultrasound, and discusses treatment options — on top of the preventive exam. Can the practice bill for both? Often, yes. But it requires the right codes, the right modifier, and documentation that clearly separates the two services.

## The rule in plain English

CPT allows a **preventive medicine visit** and a **problem-oriented E/M visit** on the same date when, during the preventive visit, a problem or abnormality is encountered — or a pre-existing problem is addressed — that's **significant enough to require additional work** to perform the key components of a problem-oriented E/M service.

When that happens:

- Bill the **preventive visit** (`99384`–`99387` new, `99394`–`99397` established).
- Bill the **problem-oriented E/M** (`99202`–`99215`) with **modifier `25`**.
- Select the problem visit level based **only on the problem work** — not the preventive history, exam or counseling.

Insignificant or trivial problems that don't require additional work aren't billed separately.

## What counts as "significant"

Examples that commonly support a separate E/M:

- New abnormal uterine bleeding requiring evaluation, testing and a management plan
- Pelvic pain requiring workup
- Management of a chronic condition such as hypertension, thyroid disease or diabetes beyond a brief mention
- A breast lump requiring evaluation and imaging orders
- Medication management for menopausal symptoms requiring decision making

Examples that usually **don't**:

- A prescription refill with no change
- A brief question answered in a sentence
- Routine screening discussion that's part of the preventive service
- Contraceptive counseling that's part of the well-woman visit

## Documentation that supports both visits

Payers increasingly audit preventive-plus-problem claims. Make each service easy to see:

- **Separate sections** or clear headings in the note for the preventive service and the problem
- For the problem: **history of the problem**, relevant **exam findings**, **assessment** and **plan** — the elements that drive medical decision making
- If billing the problem visit by **time**, document the time spent on the problem only
- A **diagnosis for each service**: preventive diagnosis (for example `Z01.419`, encounter for gynecological examination without abnormal findings, or `Z01.411` with abnormal findings) linked to the preventive code; problem diagnosis linked to the problem E/M

A useful test: if the preventive portion were removed from the note, would the remaining documentation support the problem visit level billed?

## Diagnosis coding

| Service | Example diagnosis codes |
|---|---|
| Well-woman exam, normal | `Z01.419` (gynecological exam without abnormal findings) |
| Well-woman exam, abnormal findings | `Z01.411` (with abnormal findings) + code for the abnormality |
| General adult exam | `Z00.00` / `Z00.01` |
| Problem: heavy bleeding | `N92.0` (excessive and frequent menstruation with regular cycle) or other documented `N92`/`N93` code |
| Problem: pelvic pain | `R10.2` (pelvic and perineal pain) or a more specific diagnosis |

Link each CPT code to the diagnosis that supports it.

## Medicare well-woman billing

Medicare doesn't cover routine preventive visit codes `99381`–`99397`. Instead, it covers:

- **`G0101`** — cervical or vaginal cancer screening; pelvic and clinical breast examination
- **`Q0091`** — screening Papanicolaou smear; obtaining, preparing and conveyance of specimen to laboratory
- The **Annual Wellness Visit** (`G0438`/`G0439`) for broader preventive services

`G0101` and `Q0091` are generally covered every 24 months, or every 12 months for patients at high risk. When a problem is addressed at the same visit, the problem E/M may be billed with modifier `25`. The non-covered portions of a "routine physical" may be the patient's responsibility, with an Advance Beneficiary Notice when appropriate.

For more on the pelvic exam add-on, see our [CPT 99459 guide](/blog/cpt-99459-pelvic-exam-billing).

## Patient cost-sharing: set expectations

Under the Affordable Care Act, many preventive services are covered without cost-sharing. The **problem-oriented portion is not** — it's subject to the patient's normal copay, deductible or coinsurance. Patients who expected a "free" annual exam can be surprised by a bill.

Good practice:

- Train front-desk staff to explain, at scheduling or check-in, that addressing new problems may result in a separate charge.
- Use clear signage or a short handout.
- Make sure providers document that the problem was addressed at the patient's request or due to findings.

## Modifier 25 with procedures

Modifier `25` also applies when an E/M is billed on the same day as a minor procedure — an endometrial biopsy (`58100`), colposcopy (`57452`–`57456`), IUD insertion (`58300`) or implant insertion (`11981`). Every procedure includes the usual pre-procedure evaluation, so an E/M is billable only when it's **significant and separately identifiable** — for example, evaluating a different problem, or the visit where the decision to perform the procedure was made (when the procedure is minor and payer rules allow).

## Common modifier 25 errors

1. Appending `25` automatically to every preventive-plus-E/M claim, regardless of documentation
2. Leveling the problem visit using preventive work
3. Billing a problem visit for trivial issues
4. Missing the problem visit entirely when significant work was done
5. Using `25` on the wrong line for a payer with specific instructions
6. Diagnosis pointers linking both visits to the same code

## Why this matters for audits

Modifier `25` is one of the most frequently audited modifiers across all specialties. Payers have reduced payment for E/M services billed with `25` in some policies and routinely request records. The best protection is consistent documentation and periodic internal audits by provider.
