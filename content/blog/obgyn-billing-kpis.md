"How is our billing doing?" is a hard question to answer with a feeling. It's an easy question to answer with the right numbers. The problem is that many OB/GYN practices receive reports that show activity — claims submitted, payments posted — without showing performance.

These are the twelve metrics we track for OB/GYN practices, how to calculate them, and what to do when they move in the wrong direction. A note before we start: published benchmarks vary widely by source, specialty mix and payer mix, so the most useful comparison is usually **your own trend over time**.

## 1. Net collection rate (NCR)

**Formula:** Payments ÷ (Gross charges − Contractual adjustments) × 100

**What it tells you:** How much of the money you're contractually entitled to you actually collected. Because contractual adjustments are removed, NCR isn't distorted by how high your fee schedule is.

**When it drops:** Look for unworked denials, timely-filing write-offs, underpayments, uncollected patient balances and small-balance write-offs. Use a rolling three- to six-month figure, matching payments to the charges they relate to as closely as your system allows.

## 2. Gross collection rate

**Formula:** Payments ÷ Gross charges × 100

**What it tells you:** Mostly how your fee schedule compares to what payers pay. It's not a performance measure on its own — a practice with high charges will always have a low gross collection rate. Watch it only for sudden changes.

## 3. Days in accounts receivable

**Formula:** Total A/R ÷ Average daily gross charges

(Average daily charges = last 3 months of gross charges ÷ number of days in those months.)

**What it tells you:** How long, on average, it takes to turn a charge into cash. Rising days in A/R signals slow submission, slow payers, or claims stuck in denial.

**How to reduce it:** Submit claims quickly, fix front-end errors, work denials within days, and follow up on unpaid claims before they age. See our tips in [how to reduce days in A/R](/blog/how-to-reduce-days-in-ar).

## 4. A/R over 90 days (% of total A/R)

**Formula:** A/R older than 90 days ÷ Total A/R × 100

**What it tells you:** How much of your receivables is at risk. The older a claim gets, the less likely it is to be collected — and the closer it gets to timely-filing and appeal deadlines.

**Watch for:** One or two payers holding most of your aged A/R. Breaking aged A/R down by payer and by denial reason makes the problem actionable.

## 5. First-pass acceptance rate (clean claim rate)

**Formula:** Claims paid on first submission ÷ Total claims submitted × 100

**What it tells you:** How often claims go out right the first time. Every claim that needs rework costs staff time and delays payment.

**How to improve it:** Stronger eligibility verification, claim scrubber rules for your most frequent rejection reasons, and coding feedback to providers.

## 6. Denial rate

**Formula:** Claims (or claim dollars) denied ÷ Claims (or dollars) submitted × 100

**What it tells you:** The overall volume of payer pushback. The total matters less than the **breakdown by reason** — eligibility, authorization, coding, bundling, timely filing, medical necessity — because each reason has a different fix.

## 7. Denial overturn (appeal success) rate

**Formula:** Denials successfully appealed or corrected ÷ Denials worked × 100

**What it tells you:** Whether your denial work is effective. A low overturn rate can mean appeals aren't well supported — or that many denials were correct and the real fix is upstream.

## 8. Charge lag

**Formula:** Average days from date of service to charge entry (or claim submission)

**What it tells you:** How quickly documentation becomes a claim. Long charge lag delays cash and pushes claims toward filing deadlines. Common causes: unsigned notes, coding backlogs, and surgical claims waiting for operative or pathology reports.

## 9. Patient collections at time of service

**Formula:** Patient payments collected at check-in/check-out ÷ Patient responsibility due at visit × 100

**What it tells you:** Whether your front desk is collecting copays and known balances up front. Patient balances become harder to collect after the visit, and statements cost money to send.

## 10. Open pregnancies and unbilled deliveries (OB-specific)

**What to track:** The number of active pregnancies by payer and expected delivery date, deliveries that haven't been billed, and patients who transferred out without an antepartum claim.

**Why it matters:** Under global billing (through 2026), obstetric revenue isn't visible in standard A/R until the claim is submitted. A practice can look healthy on A/R while thousands of dollars of obstetric care sit unbilled. See [global maternity billing](/global-maternity-billing).

## 11. Time from delivery to claim submission (OB-specific)

**What to track:** Average days from delivery date to submission of the delivery (or global) claim.

**Why it matters:** Practices sometimes hold global claims until the postpartum visit, which can push them close to commercial timely-filing limits. Know each payer's policy and submit as early as it allows.

## 12. Surgical claims pending documentation

**What to track:** Hysterectomy and other surgical claims waiting for operative notes or pathology reports, and how long they've waited.

**Why it matters:** Uterine weight determines the hysterectomy code tier, so holding claims for pathology is correct — but they shouldn't wait indefinitely. A short list reviewed weekly keeps them moving. See [GYN surgery coding](/gyn-surgery-coding).

## Building a simple monthly KPI report

You don't need a fancy dashboard. One page, every month, with:

Add a short note on what changed and what you're doing about it. The note is often more valuable than the numbers.

## Turning KPIs into action

- **NCR down + denials up** → work the denial breakdown by reason; fix the top two causes first.
- **Days in A/R up + denials flat** → check charge lag and payer payment timing; a single slow payer is common.
- **A/R over 90 days rising** → review aged claims against filing and appeal deadlines immediately.
- **First-pass rate falling** → add claim scrubber rules for the most common rejections; check for a recent payer policy change.
- **Unbilled deliveries growing** → review the pregnancy registry and delivery claim workflow.

## Common reporting traps

KPIs are only as good as the data behind them. Watch for these distortions:

- **Mixing time periods.** Comparing this month's payments to this month's charges ignores payment lag. Use rolling averages or match payments to the charges they pay.
- **Credit balances and refunds** that inflate or deflate A/R. Clean them up regularly.
- **Write-offs coded as contractual adjustments.** If denials or timely-filing losses are written off as "contractual," your NCR will look better than reality. Use separate adjustment codes for each reason.
- **Global OB claims not yet submitted.** As noted above, they don't appear in A/R at all.
- **Small-balance write-off policies** that quietly absorb patient balances.
- **Payer-level averages hiding provider-level problems.** Look at denials by rendering provider too.

A reliable KPI report starts with consistent adjustment codes and a clear month-end process. If your current reports don't separate contractual adjustments from write-offs, that's the first thing to fix.
