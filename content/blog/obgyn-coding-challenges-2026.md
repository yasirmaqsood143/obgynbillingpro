OB/GYN coders often say their specialty is really three specialties in one: obstetrics, gynecologic surgery and office gynecology — each with different rules, plus a diagnosis chapter of its own. Add payer variation and a once-in-a-generation code overhaul arriving on January 1, 2027, and it's no surprise OB/GYN coding is one of the hardest areas of medical billing to get consistently right.

Here are the ten challenges we see most often in 2026, and practical ways to solve each.

## 1. Global maternity package vs. components

**The challenge:** Choosing between global codes (`59400`, `59510`, `59610`, `59618`) and component codes when care is split by transfers, insurance changes or deliveries outside the group.

**The fix:** A pregnancy registry from the first visit — dates, visit count, payer at each visit, delivering provider — and a reconciliation that confirms every pregnancy ends in a billed package, billed components, or a documented reason. See [split maternity care billing](/blog/split-maternity-care-billing-transfer-example).

## 2. The 2027 maternity transition

**The challenge:** The global package ends January 1, 2027. Prenatal and postpartum visits become per-encounter E/M services, and labor and delivery get new codes. Pregnancies that span the change need careful handling.

**The fix:** Start now — inventory pregnancies due in 2027, update charge capture and templates, rebuild fee schedules and ask payers how they'll handle the transition. Our [2027 maternity transition guide](/resources/2027-maternity-transition) has a checklist.

## 3. Hysterectomy weight tiers and adnexal codes

**The challenge:** Laparoscopic, vaginal and LAVH hysterectomy codes split at 250 grams and by whether tubes/ovaries were removed. Billing before pathology, or missing an opportunistic salpingectomy, undercodes the case.

**The fix:** Hold hysterectomy claims for pathology, and read the full operative note for adnexal work. See [CPT code for robotic hysterectomy](/blog/cpt-code-robotic-hysterectomy).

## 4. Modifier 25 on preventive and procedure visits

**The challenge:** Deciding when a problem addressed at a well-woman visit — or an evaluation on the day of an IUD insertion or endometrial biopsy — supports a separate E/M with modifier `25`.

**The fix:** Clear documentation of the separate problem and the decision making it required, plus consistent internal rules. Overuse invites audits; underuse leaves legitimate revenue unbilled. Read our [modifier 25 guide](/blog/modifier-25-well-woman-visit).

## 5. NCCI bundling in gynecologic surgery

**The challenge:** Lysis of adhesions, endometriosis excision, cystoscopy and diagnostic procedures are frequently bundled into primary procedures. Modifiers `59` and `XS` are often applied by habit.

**The fix:** Scrub against NCCI edits before submission and require documentation of distinct sites or sessions for any bypass modifier. When extra work is real but not distinct, modifier `22` with documentation is often the better choice. See our [modifier 59 and X-modifier guide](/blog/modifier-59-xs-gyn-surgery-ncci).

## 6. Chapter 15 diagnosis sequencing

**The challenge:** O codes take priority, trimester characters depend on the date of service, seventh characters identify fetuses, and `Z34`, `Z3A`, `Z37` and `O80` each have rules that are easy to break.

**The fix:** Claim scrubber edits for the common errors, and provider templates that update diagnoses at each visit instead of copying them forward. See [multiple O codes sequencing](/blog/multiple-o-codes-sequencing-pregnancy).

## 7. E/M documentation under MDM and time

**The challenge:** E/M levels are selected by medical decision making or total time. Templated notes often don't capture the complexity of a problem visit — especially in pregnancy — and some notes overstate it.

**The fix:** Provider education with real examples from your own notes, and regular E/M audits by provider. This becomes critical in 2027, when every prenatal visit is billed by E/M level.

## 8. Telehealth rules that differ by payer

**The challenge:** POS `02` vs. `10`, modifiers `95` and `93`, the telemedicine E/M codes `98000`–`98016`, and audio-only coverage all vary by payer — and Medicare's flexibilities are extended only through December 31, 2027.

**The fix:** A payer-by-payer telehealth rule table built into your scrubber. See [modifier 95 vs 93 vs GT](/blog/modifier-95-vs-93-vs-gt-telehealth).

## 9. LARC devices and office supplies

**The challenge:** Insertions billed without device codes, devices billed when a pharmacy supplied them, wrong J-codes for the brand, and postpartum LARC device confusion between practice and hospital.

**The fix:** Device inventory reconciliation — devices received, used, billed and paid — every month. See our [LARC billing guide](/blog/larc-billing-iud-nexplanon-codes).

## 10. Payer-specific policies

**The challenge:** Medicaid managed care plans, Blue Cross Blue Shield licensees and commercial payers each publish their own obstetric, surgical, ultrasound and telehealth policies — and update them without much notice.

**The fix:** A maintained payer rule library for your top payers, reviewed quarterly, with denial trends used to spot policy changes early.

## A simple OB/GYN coding quality program

You don't need a large compliance department to keep coding accurate. A practical program includes:

1. **Pre-bill edits** for the most common errors (claim scrubber rules)
2. **Monthly denial review** by reason and provider
3. **Quarterly focused audits** — one topic at a time (for example modifier `25`, hysterectomy coding, LARC)
4. **Annual provider-level audit** with feedback
5. **Payer policy review** quarterly for your top payers
6. **New-provider review** of the first months of claims

## Where to find reliable references

- The current **CPT codebook** and its guidelines, especially the maternity care section
- The **ICD-10-CM Official Guidelines** for Coding and Reporting (updated every October 1)
- **NCCI** procedure-to-procedure edits and the NCCI Policy Manual
- Your **payers' published medical and reimbursement policies**
- Professional society coding resources, such as ACOG's coding guidance
