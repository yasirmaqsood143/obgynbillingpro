Search for "OB/GYN revenue cycle management" and you'll find two very different things: **software** that helps your staff manage billing, and **services** where an outside team does the billing for you. Both get called "RCM," and vendors don't always make the difference clear.

This guide explains what RCM software actually does, which features matter specifically for OB/GYN, what to ask in a demo, and how to decide whether your practice needs better software, more skilled people, or both.

## What RCM software does — and doesn't do

Revenue cycle management software (often part of a practice management, or PM, system) is the toolset billing staff use every day. Typical features include:

- **Patient registration and eligibility checks** — electronic verification against payer databases
- **Charge entry** — turning documented services into billable line items, often fed from the EHR
- **Claim scrubbing** — automated edits that catch errors before claims go out
- **Clearinghouse connectivity** — electronic claim submission, rejections and remittances
- **Payment posting** — electronic remittance advice (ERA) posted to accounts
- **Denial and A/R work queues** — lists of claims needing attention
- **Patient statements and online payments**
- **Reporting and dashboards**

What software **doesn't** do is the judgment-heavy work: deciding which global maternity component codes apply after a patient transfers, reading an operative note to choose between `58570` and `58571`, writing a persuasive appeal, or calling a payer about an underpaid claim. Software organizes the work. People still do it.

## Features OB/GYN practices should look for

General-purpose PM systems can bill OB/GYN, but some features make a real difference for women's health:

### Pregnancy tracking

A pregnancy record that captures the first visit date, estimated delivery date, antepartum visit count, payer at each visit and delivering provider. Without it, global maternity billing becomes a manual reconstruction at delivery time.

### 2027 maternity code readiness

On January 1, 2027, CPT deletes the global maternity codes and introduces per-encounter prenatal billing plus new labor management and delivery codes. Ask every vendor how their system will handle the transition, including pregnancies that span it. See our [2027 maternity transition guide](/resources/2027-maternity-transition).

### OB/GYN claim scrubber rules

Edits for O-code sequencing, `Z37` on delivery claims, `Z34` combined with O codes, hysterectomy weight tiers, LARC insertion without a device code, and payer-specific modifier rules.

### Ultrasound and in-office procedure capture

Templates and charge capture that make it easy to bill `76801`–`76817`, endometrial biopsies, colposcopies and LARC procedures correctly.

### Reporting you can actually use

Net collection rate, A/R aging by payer, denial reasons, charge lag and open pregnancies — not just "claims submitted." Our article on [OB/GYN billing KPIs](/blog/obgyn-billing-kpis) explains what to track.

### Cloud-based access and security

Cloud-based (browser-based) systems are now the norm. They're accessible from any device, including tablets, and the vendor handles updates. Make sure the vendor signs a Business Associate Agreement, supports multi-factor authentication and individual user accounts, and keeps audit logs.

## Questions to ask in an RCM software demo

Demos are designed to look smooth. These questions get you past the script:

1. **"Show me how you'd bill a patient who transferred in at 24 weeks and delivered by cesarean."** Watch how the system handles component codes.
2. **"How will the system handle the 2027 maternity codes — and patients who start prenatal care in 2026 and deliver in 2027?"**
3. **"What happens when a claim rejects at the clearinghouse? Who sees it and where?"**
4. **"Show me your denial work queue sorted by denial reason and dollar value."**
5. **"Can I see net collection rate and A/R over 90 days by payer without exporting to a spreadsheet?"**
6. **"Which claim scrubber edits are OB/GYN-specific? Can we add our own rules?"**
7. **"What does implementation include, how long does it take, and what's the total cost — including clearinghouse fees, per-claim fees and add-on modules?"**
8. **"Will you sign a BAA, and how do you handle data export if we leave?"**

Ask for a walkthrough on **your** scenarios, not the vendor's sample patients.

## Software vs. outsourced RCM: an honest comparison

| | RCM software (in-house team) | Outsourced RCM service |
|---|---|---|
| **What you get** | Tools | People + process (using your tools or theirs) |
| **Who does the work** | Your staff | The billing company's team |
| **Specialty expertise** | Depends on whom you hire | Depends on the company — ask about OB/GYN specifically |
| **Cost** | Subscription + staff salaries, benefits, training | Usually a percentage of collections or flat fee |
| **Coverage** | Pauses when staff are out or leave | Team-based coverage |
| **Control** | Direct | Shared, through reports and system access |
| **Best for** | Practices with experienced, stable billing staff | Practices without specialized billing staff, or with turnover |

Here's the pattern we see most often: a practice already owns capable software through its EHR, but **the bottleneck is people**. One overworked biller can't keep up with denials, A/R follow-up and payer changes. Buying another software product doesn't fix that. More specialized staff time does — whether that's hiring, training or outsourcing.

## When better software is the right answer

- Your billing team is experienced and stable, but the current system makes simple tasks slow
- Your system lacks pregnancy tracking or can't handle the 2027 maternity codes
- Reporting requires hours of spreadsheet work every month
- Your clearinghouse connection is unreliable or expensive

## When outsourcing is the right answer

- You don't have a dedicated, experienced OB/GYN biller
- Denials and aged A/R keep growing despite good software
- Billing stops whenever one person is on vacation
- You're opening a new practice and don't want to build a billing department
- You want specialist support for one area — denials, coding audits, credentialing — while keeping the rest in-house

## The hybrid approach

Many practices end up with both: their existing PM system plus an outside team that works inside it. You keep ownership of your data and your software, and the billing company provides the specialized staff time. That's how we work with most of our clients — inside athenahealth, eClinicalWorks, NextGen, AdvancedMD, Tebra, DrChrono, ModMed and other systems.

## How to evaluate an outsourced RCM partner

If you're considering outsourcing, ask:

- How much **OB/GYN-specific** experience does the team have?
- Will they work **inside our existing system**?
- What will we see in **monthly reports**?
- How are **denials** handled — every one worked and categorized?
- What does it **cost**, and what's excluded?
- What happens if we **part ways**?
- Will they **sign a BAA**?

Be wary of anyone promising specific results before they've looked at your data.

## Total cost of ownership: what to count

Comparing software to outsourcing on price alone is misleading unless you count everything:

**In-house with software:**
- Software subscription or license, per-provider fees, add-on modules
- Clearinghouse fees (per claim or monthly), ERA/EFT fees, patient statement fees
- Biller and coder salaries, benefits and payroll taxes
- Training, coding resources and continuing education
- Turnover — recruiting, onboarding and the revenue impact of a vacancy
- Management time spent supervising billing

**Outsourced:**
- The billing company's fee (percentage of collections or flat)
- Any items not included in the fee — ask specifically about appeals, patient statements, credentialing and reporting
- Internal staff time still needed for front-desk work, charge entry or documentation

Lay these out side by side for a full year. The answer is different for every practice — and sometimes the cheapest option on paper costs the most once collections are included.
