Authorization denials are among the most frustrating in OB/GYN billing because the care was appropriate, documented and often approved in principle — but a required step was missed, or the approved code didn't match what was billed. This reference covers the services most likely to need prior authorization and how to build a process that prevents `CO-197` denials.

*Requirements vary by payer, plan and state, and they change. Always verify with the specific plan before the service.*

## Surgical procedures

| Procedure | Typical codes | Common requirements |
|---|---|---|
| Hysterectomy (benign) | `58150`, `58260`–`58294`, `58541`–`58554`, `58570`–`58573` | Clinical criteria: symptoms, failed conservative treatment, imaging |
| Myomectomy | `58140`–`58146`, `58545`, `58546`, `58561` | Fibroid documentation, symptoms |
| Laparoscopic adnexal surgery | `58661`, `58662` | Imaging findings, indication |
| Endometrial ablation | `58353`, `58356`, `58563` | Documentation of bleeding and prior treatment |
| Pelvic floor / incontinence surgery | `57240`–`57288`, `57425` | Symptom severity, conservative treatment history |
| Sacral neuromodulation, bladder chemodenervation | `64561`, `64590`, `52287` | Failed first- and second-line therapy |
| Gender-affirming or cosmetic procedures | Various | Plan-specific criteria |

**The CPT match matters.** If the authorization lists `58570` and pathology confirms a 300 g uterus, the billed code becomes `58572` — which may not be covered by the authorization. When imaging suggests a large uterus, request authorization for the higher-tier code or both, per payer rules. If adnexal removal is added intraoperatively, update the authorization promptly.

## Infertility treatment

- Ovulation induction monitoring, IUI and IVF frequently require authorization when they're covered at all
- Fertility benefit managers often run their own authorization process
- Fertility preservation before cancer treatment may need urgent authorization — many plans have expedited review

See [infertility & REI billing](/infertility-rei-billing) and our [IUI and IVF CPT codes reference](/blog/iui-ivf-cpt-codes).

## Advanced imaging

- **Pelvic MRI** and **CT** are commonly managed by radiology benefit managers
- **Fetal MRI** may require authorization
- Routine **obstetric ultrasound** usually doesn't need authorization, but payers limit frequency; additional studies need documented indications

## Genetic and prenatal testing

Cell-free DNA screening, carrier screening and diagnostic genetic tests often have coverage criteria and may require authorization or a laboratory benefit manager review. The ordering practice and the laboratory should coordinate on who obtains authorization.

## Injectable medications

Office-administered drugs — for example, certain hormonal treatments or iron infusions — may require authorization under the medical benefit, or may need to be obtained through the pharmacy benefit instead. Confirm the benefit before ordering.

## Obstetric notifications

Some Medicaid programs and managed care plans require **pregnancy notification** early in prenatal care, and some require authorization or notification for hospital admission for delivery. Missing a notification can affect payment for the obstetric package. See [global maternity billing by payer](/blog/global-maternity-billing-by-payer).

## Building an authorization checklist by payer

1. **List your top payers** and the services you perform most.
2. **Record each payer's requirements** — which codes, what documentation, submission method, turnaround time, validity period.
3. **Build it into scheduling** so surgical and imaging bookings trigger an authorization check automatically.
4. **Track every authorization** — number, approved codes and units, date range, facility.
5. **Match authorization to claim** before submission.
6. **Plan for changes** — know each payer's window for post-procedure updates.

## What to include in an authorization request

- Diagnosis codes and clinical indication
- Relevant history: symptoms, duration, prior treatments and their results
- Imaging and lab results
- Planned procedure codes and setting (office, ASC, hospital)
- Provider and facility NPIs

Complete requests get decisions faster. Incomplete ones generate requests for information that delay surgery.

## When authorization is denied

- Request a **peer-to-peer** review when the payer offers one
- Submit additional documentation addressing the specific criteria not met
- Appeal formally if needed
- Discuss options with the patient, including financial responsibility, before proceeding without authorization

## Key takeaways

- Surgery, infertility, advanced imaging, genetic testing and injectables are the most common OB/GYN authorization categories.
- Authorization must match the billed CPT code — plan for weight tiers and intraoperative changes.
- A payer-specific checklist built into scheduling prevents most `CO-197` denials.

## A sample authorization workflow

1. **Scheduling:** when a procedure is booked, the scheduler records the planned CPT codes, facility, date and payer.
2. **Requirement check:** the verification team checks the payer's authorization list for those codes and that setting.
3. **Request:** if required, the request is submitted with clinical notes, imaging and prior treatment history.
4. **Tracking:** the request is logged with a follow-up date; the surgery date is protected until approval.
5. **Decision:** approval details (number, codes, units, dates) are recorded in the patient's account; denials go to peer-to-peer or appeal.
6. **Day of surgery:** the team confirms the authorization is still valid for the date and codes.
7. **After surgery:** if the procedure changed, an update is requested within the payer's window before the claim is submitted.

## Common authorization mistakes

- Assuming that a payer that didn't require authorization last year doesn't require it now
- Getting authorization for the surgeon but not the facility (or vice versa) when both are required
- Letting authorizations expire when surgery is rescheduled
- Authorizing a diagnostic procedure that becomes therapeutic during the case
- Not checking whether the patient's plan uses a separate benefit manager for imaging or fertility
