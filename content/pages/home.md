## What OB/GYN medical billing services actually include

"Medical billing" covers a lot of ground, and OB/GYN practices need more of it than most specialties. A complete OB/GYN billing service should handle the whole revenue cycle:

- **Insurance eligibility and benefits verification** before visits, deliveries and surgeries — including re-verification during pregnancy, when coverage often changes.
- **Prior authorization** for hysterectomy, myomectomy, imaging and other services payers require it for.
- **Charge capture and coding** for obstetric packages and components, office gynecology, preventive visits, ultrasounds, LARC procedures and gynecologic surgery.
- **Claim scrubbing and submission**, with OB/GYN-specific edits for O-code sequencing, `Z37` outcome codes, modifiers and NCCI bundling.
- **Payment posting and underpayment review** against your contracted rates.
- **Denial management and appeals**, worked by root cause so the same denials stop recurring.
- **A/R follow-up** on every unpaid claim before timely-filing limits run out.
- **Patient billing** with clear statements and courteous support.
- **Credentialing and enrollment** so every provider can be paid by every payer you accept.
- **Reporting** that shows your collections, A/R, denial reasons and open pregnancies in plain English.

We provide all of it, or the pieces you need — some practices keep billing in-house and use us for [denial management](/denial-management), [coding audits](/coding-audits) or [credentialing](/credentialing) only.

## Why a specialized OB/GYN billing company?

General billing companies can submit claims. What they often miss are the specialty-specific details that decide whether an OB/GYN claim is paid in full:

- **Global maternity rules.** Which services are inside the package, when to bill components instead, how to split billing when a patient changes insurance or providers mid-pregnancy, and how to bill VBAC and multiple gestations. See [global maternity billing](/global-maternity-billing).
- **Gynecologic surgery coding.** Uterine weight thresholds, approach, adnexal procedures, NCCI edits and the modifiers that make or break a surgical claim. See [GYN surgery coding](/gyn-surgery-coding).
- **Chapter 15 diagnosis coding.** O codes have their own sequencing rules, trimester characters and companion Z codes. See our [ICD-10 O codes guide](/resources/icd10-o-codes).
- **The 2027 maternity transition.** On January 1, 2027, the global obstetric package is replaced by phase-based reporting. A billing partner who isn't preparing for it now will be learning on your claims next year. See our [2027 transition guide](/resources/2027-maternity-transition).

## In-house billing vs. outsourcing: an honest comparison

| | In-house billing | Outsourced OB/GYN billing |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost structure** | Salaries, benefits, training, software seats, turnover | Usually a percentage of collections or a flat fee |
| **Specialty expertise** | Depends on the individual you hire | Team that bills OB/GYN every day |
| **Coverage** | Vacations and sick days pause billing | Team coverage keeps claims moving |
| **Control** | Direct supervision | Shared visibility through reports and your PM system |
| **Scalability** | Hire as you grow | Scales with volume |

In-house billing works well for some practices, especially larger groups with an experienced billing manager. Outsourcing tends to work best for solo and small-group practices that can't justify a full specialized billing team, and for practices where one key biller has left. Our [revenue cycle management](/revenue-cycle-management) page explains how we work alongside your staff, and our [revenue leakage calculator](/calculator) can help you estimate what's at stake.

## How OB/GYN billing companies are priced

Most OB/GYN billing companies charge one of two ways:

1. **Percentage of collections** — you pay a percentage of what we collect on your behalf. Our incentives are aligned with yours: we're paid when you're paid.
2. **Flat monthly fee** — predictable cost, usually set by provider count and volume.

Be cautious of very low percentages that exclude essential work like denial follow-up, appeals or patient billing — or that charge extra for each. We quote after the free audit, when we understand your volume, payer mix and needs, and we don't charge setup fees.
