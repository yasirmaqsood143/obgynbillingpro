## The biggest obstetric coding change in decades

For as long as most practices have billed obstetric care, a routine pregnancy has been reported with a single global code — `59400` for a vaginal delivery or `59510` for a cesarean — submitted after the baby was born. That model ends on **January 1, 2027**.

The American Medical Association's CPT Editorial Panel restructured the maternity care section to replace the global package with **phase-based reporting**: antepartum, labor, delivery and postpartum care are each reported on their own. The AMA released the CPT 2027 code set in September 2026, and the maternity section accounts for 35 code changes — **17 deleted codes, 12 new codes and 6 revised codes**.

For OB/GYN practices, this isn't a simple crosswalk. It changes when you bill, what you document, how your front desk works, and how your payer contracts pay you.

## What's deleted

Per published summaries of the CPT 2027 changes, the 17 deleted codes are:

| Code family | Deleted codes |
|---|---|
| Vaginal delivery — global, delivery only, delivery + postpartum | `59400`, `59409`, `59410` |
| Cesarean — global, delivery only, delivery + postpartum | `59510`, `59514`, `59515` |
| VBAC — global, delivery only, delivery + postpartum | `59610`, `59612`, `59614` |
| Attempted VBAC ending in cesarean | `59618`, `59620`, `59622` |
| Antepartum care only | `59425`, `59426` |
| Postpartum care only | `59430` |
| Other | `59050`, `59525` |

These codes are **not valid for dates of service on or after January 1, 2027**. Always confirm the final list against the AMA CPT 2027 Professional Edition.

## What's new

### Labor management — new codes, reported per day

| Code | Descriptor (summary) |
|---|---|
| `59080` | Initial day of labor management, straightforward |
| `59081` | Initial day of labor management, complex |
| `59082` | Subsequent day of labor management, straightforward |
| `59083` | Subsequent day of labor management, complex |

According to the AMA's guidance, labor is **complex if any of the straightforward criteria isn't met**, multiple gestations are complex by default, and only one complex code is reported per calendar date regardless of the number of fetuses. **Planned cesarean deliveries have no labor management code.** Hospital E/M services by the same clinician stop once labor management begins that day.

### Delivery — new codes, reported once per delivery event

| Code | Descriptor (summary) |
|---|---|
| `59431` | Vaginal delivery, with or without episiotomy |
| `59432` | Vaginal delivery after previous cesarean delivery (VBAC) |
| `59502` | Primary cesarean delivery |
| `59503` | Repeat cesarean delivery |
| `59504` | Cesarean hysterectomy |
| `59433`, `59434` | Third- and fourth-degree laceration repair, reported with the delivery code |
| `59623` | Uterine tamponade for postpartum hemorrhage (balloon, catheter, vacuum or packing) |

First- and second-degree laceration repair by a clinician other than the one who performed the delivery is reported with `59300`.

## How each phase of care will be reported

- **Antepartum (prenatal) care:** each visit is reported with the appropriate E/M code for the setting — office, hospital or telehealth — selected by medical decision making or total time. There's no fixed level across a patient's visits; each encounter stands on its own.
- **Labor:** new per-day labor management codes.
- **Delivery:** new delivery codes reported once per delivery event. **Routine same-day postpartum care is included** in the delivery code.
- **Postpartum care:** later inpatient days use subsequent hospital E/M codes followed by discharge day management; outpatient postpartum visits use office E/M codes.

Payers will be able to identify pregnancy-related claims using `Z34` or Chapter 15 O codes, and the HCPCS modifier `TH` where applicable — which makes accurate [O-code diagnosis coding](/resources/icd10-o-codes) even more important.

## What this means for your practice

### Cash flow changes — for the better, mostly

Under the global package, a practice might provide months of prenatal care before submitting a single claim. Per-encounter billing means prenatal visits can be billed as they happen. That should smooth cash flow — but only if your charge capture and claim workflow are ready to handle many more obstetric claims.

### Documentation matters at every visit

When each prenatal visit is a separately billed E/M service, each note must support the level billed. Templated "routine OB visit" notes that worked fine inside a global package may not support the E/M levels your providers expect. Provider education on MDM and time-based documentation should start now.

### Patient cost-sharing will look different

Patients who previously saw one bill after delivery may now see copays, deductibles or coinsurance applied to individual prenatal visits, depending on their plan. Front-desk staff need a clear script for explaining this, and financial policies may need updating.

### Payer contracts need review

Many contracts specify rates for `59400`, `59510` and related codes, or pay obstetric care as a case rate. Those terms don't automatically translate to the new codes. Practices should ask each major payer how it will reimburse the new structure and review the financial impact. See our [fee schedule optimization](/fee-schedule-optimization) service.

### Group and team-based care

The new structure better reflects care shared among physicians, midwives and other clinicians — each clinician can report the services they personally perform. Groups should confirm how rendering providers will be assigned and how payers will treat services by different clinicians on the same date.

### Pregnancies that span the change

A patient whose prenatal care begins in 2026 and who delivers in 2027 spans both systems. Watch for payer-specific transition instructions. The safest preparation is to document every antepartum visit — date, rendering provider, payer — in a way that lets you bill under whichever method each payer specifies.

## Your 2027 readiness checklist

1. **Inventory your open pregnancies** by expected delivery date and payer.
2. **Update your PM/EHR** — new codes loaded, deleted codes inactivated for 2027 dates of service, charge forms rebuilt.
3. **Rebuild your fee schedule** for the new codes.
4. **Contact your top payers** about reimbursement for the new codes and transition rules for in-progress pregnancies.
5. **Train providers** on E/M documentation for prenatal and postpartum visits and on the labor management criteria.
6. **Train front-desk staff** on per-visit cost-sharing and updated financial counseling.
7. **Update claim scrubber rules** — for example, delivery claims without required diagnosis codes, or labor management billed with a planned cesarean.
8. **Monitor early 2027 claims closely** for denials and payer processing errors.
