## Why gynecologic surgery coding is different

A single gynecologic surgery claim can be worth more than a month of office visits. It can also be undercoded by a full RVU tier because one detail — uterine weight, the approach, whether the ovaries were removed — didn't make it from the operative note onto the claim.

GYN surgical coding sits at the intersection of three hard problems:

1. **Code selection is detail-driven.** The same hysterectomy can map to a dozen different CPT codes depending on approach (abdominal, vaginal, laparoscopic, laparoscopically assisted vaginal), uterine weight, and whether tubes and ovaries were removed.
2. **Bundling is aggressive.** National Correct Coding Initiative (NCCI) edits bundle many of the procedures that commonly happen alongside a hysterectomy — lysis of adhesions, lesion excision, diagnostic laparoscopy — unless there's a documented reason they're distinct.
3. **Payers scrutinize high-dollar claims.** Modifier 22, modifier 59 and assistant-surgeon claims are frequent audit targets.

We've spent more than eight years coding OB/GYN surgery. The approach is simple: read the whole operative note and the pathology report, code what's documented, and don't append a modifier unless the documentation will defend it.

## Hysterectomy CPT codes at a glance

| Approach | ≤ 250 g | ≤ 250 g + tubes/ovaries | > 250 g | > 250 g + tubes/ovaries |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total laparoscopic (TLH) | `58570` | `58571` | `58572` | `58573` |
| Laparoscopic supracervical (LSH) | `58541` | `58542` | `58543` | `58544` |
| Laparoscopically assisted vaginal (LAVH) | `58550` | `58552` | `58553` | `58554` |
| Vaginal | `58260` | `58262` | `58290` | `58291` |

Abdominal approaches use different codes: `58150` for a total abdominal hysterectomy with or without removal of tubes and ovaries, `58180` for supracervical, and `58200`/`58210` for more extensive and radical procedures.

**Robotic cases** are coded with the matching laparoscopic code. There's no robotic-specific CPT code. A few commercial payers recognize HCPCS `S2900` as an add-on for robotic assistance, but it's not universally paid — we check payer policy before reporting it. For a deeper walkthrough, see our guide to the [CPT code for robotic hysterectomy](/blog/cpt-code-robotic-hysterectomy).

## Myomectomy, adnexal and other common GYN procedures

**Myomectomy** codes depend on approach and on the number and total weight of fibroids removed:

- Abdominal: `58140` (1–4 intramural myomas, total weight 250 g or less, and/or surface myomas) and `58146` (5 or more, or total weight over 250 g)
- Vaginal approach: `58145`
- Laparoscopic: `58545` (1–4 intramural myomas, 250 g or less) and `58546` (5 or more, or over 250 g)
- Hysteroscopic: `58561`

**Adnexal and laparoscopic procedures** include `58661` (laparoscopic removal of adnexal structures — partial or total oophorectomy and/or salpingectomy), `58662` (laparoscopic fulguration or excision of lesions of the ovary, pelvic viscera or peritoneal surface — commonly used for endometriosis), `58670`/`58671` (laparoscopic tubal sterilization) and `49320` (diagnostic laparoscopy, which is bundled into surgical laparoscopy).

**Hysteroscopy:** `58555` (diagnostic), `58558` (with endometrial sampling and/or polypectomy), `58561` (myomectomy), `58562` (foreign body removal) and `58563` (endometrial ablation).

**Cervical procedures:** colposcopy `57452`–`57461` (including LEEP with colposcopy, `57460`, and conization, `57461`), and cold-knife or LEEP conization without colposcopy (`57520`, `57522`).

**Pelvic floor and prolapse:** `57240` (anterior colporrhaphy), `57250` (posterior colporrhaphy), `57260`/`57265` (combined repair, with enterocele repair), `57288` (sling) and `57425` (laparoscopic colpopexy). Complex pelvic floor work is covered on our [urogynecology billing](/urogynecology-billing) page.

## Modifiers we apply — and when

Modifiers are where good surgical coding is most visible. Used correctly, they get legitimate secondary procedures paid. Used loosely, they trigger audits and recoupments.

- **51 — Multiple procedures.** Identifies additional procedures in the same session; payers apply multiple-procedure payment reductions.
- **59 / XE / XS / XP / XU — Distinct procedural service.** Only when NCCI bundles two codes and the documentation shows a separate session, site, lesion or practitioner. The X-modifiers are more specific and preferred by many payers.
- **22 — Increased procedural services.** For substantially greater work, documented in the operative note with the reason and ideally the extra time.
- **62 — Co-surgeons.** Two surgeons each performing distinct parts of one procedure.
- **80 / 82 / AS — Assistant at surgery.** Only when the payer's assistant-surgeon indicator allows it for that code.
- **58 / 78 / 79 — Global period modifiers.** Staged or related procedures, unplanned returns to the OR, and unrelated procedures during a post-op period.
- **50 — Bilateral.** Only when the code's bilateral surgery indicator allows it. For example, `58661` carries a Medicare bilateral indicator of 1 (modifier `50` applies when both sides are treated), while `58662` and `58700` don't take modifier `50`. Hysterectomy codes "with removal of tube(s) and/or ovary(s)" never take it. Some commercial payers instruct otherwise, so we follow each payer's policy.

## Diagnosis coding for gynecologic surgery

Medical necessity edits look at the ICD-10-CM codes on the claim. The most common surgical diagnoses we see include:

- `D25.-` — leiomyoma of uterus (fibroids), coded by location
- `N80.-` — endometriosis, with the expanded 2023+ codes that specify site and depth
- `N81.-` — female genital prolapse
- `N83.-` — noninflammatory disorders of the ovary, fallopian tube and broad ligament (including ovarian cysts)
- `N87.-` — dysplasia of the cervix
- `N92.-` / `N93.-` — abnormal uterine bleeding
- `N94.-` — pelvic pain and related conditions

The diagnosis should match the reason for the procedure — and the pathology report. A hysterectomy billed only with a pelvic pain code is far more likely to draw a medical-necessity denial than one coded to the fibroids or adenomyosis the pathology confirms.

## Prior authorization and payer policies

Many commercial and Medicaid plans require prior authorization for hysterectomy, myomectomy and some laparoscopic procedures, and several publish clinical criteria for hysterectomy for benign disease. An authorization for the wrong CPT code — for instance, authorized as `58570` when the procedure became a `58571` — can cause a denial even though the surgery was approved. Our [eligibility verification](/eligibility-verification) team confirms authorizations match the planned procedure, and we update them when the surgical plan changes.

## Global surgical periods

Most major gynecologic surgeries carry a 90-day global period, and many minor procedures carry a 0- or 10-day global. During that time, routine post-op visits aren't separately billable — but unrelated problems, complications that require a return to the OR, and staged procedures may be. We track every patient's global period so post-op visits aren't billed in error and legitimate services inside the global aren't missed.

## Our GYN surgery coding process

1. **Documentation review.** We read the full operative note, not just the procedure line, plus the pathology report.
2. **Code selection.** Approach, weight, adnexa and every additional procedure mapped to CPT and ICD-10-CM.
3. **NCCI and payer edits.** Every code pair checked; modifiers applied only when supported.
4. **Provider feedback.** When a note doesn't support the highest-value accurate code, we tell the surgeon what was missing so the next note is complete.
5. **Follow-up and appeals.** Surgical denials are appealed with the operative note and a clear explanation, not just resubmitted. See our [denial management](/denial-management) service.

We also offer standalone [coding audits](/coding-audits) if you want an independent review of surgical coding done in-house or by another vendor.

## Documentation tips for surgeons

- State the approach clearly, including conversions (for example, laparoscopic converted to open)
- List every procedure performed, with laterality for adnexal procedures
- Describe lesion sites and sizes for endometriosis excision or fulguration
- Note adhesions, their density and the time spent if they significantly increased the work
- Identify assistants and why an assistant was medically necessary
