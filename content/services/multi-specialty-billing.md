## Cardiology billing

Cardiology is one of the most coding-intensive specialties, with frequent professional/technical component splits, diagnostic testing with strict medical necessity rules, and procedures with complex bundling. We handle:

### Diagnostic testing

| Service | Example codes |
|---|---|
| Electrocardiogram | `93000`, `93005`, `93010` |
| Transthoracic echocardiography | `93306`, `93307`, `93308` |
| Stress testing | `93015`–`93018`, with stress echo `93350`/`93351` |
| Holter and extended ECG monitoring | `93224`–`93227`, `93241`–`93248` |
| Event and mobile cardiac telemetry | `93268`–`93272`, `93228`/`93229` |
| Vascular studies | `93880`, `93970`, `93922`–`93924` |

### Device management

Pacemaker and defibrillator interrogation and programming (`93279`–`93298`) — in-person and remote — with the correct global period, frequency and professional/technical splits.

### Procedures and hospital services

Cardiac catheterization, interventional procedures, and inpatient and observation E/M services, coded from the procedure report with attention to bundled imaging and add-on codes.

### Cardiology-specific challenges we manage

- **Medical necessity.** Local and national coverage determinations specify which diagnoses support echoes, stress tests and monitoring. We check diagnosis linkage before submission.
- **Component billing.** Practices that read studies performed at hospitals bill the professional component (modifier `26`); global billing applies only when the practice owns the equipment and performs both components.
- **Frequency limits.** Repeat echocardiograms and monitoring services face payer frequency edits that need documented justification.
- **Prior authorization.** Advanced imaging such as nuclear stress testing and cardiac CT/MRI frequently requires authorization through radiology benefit managers.

## Internal medicine and family practice

Primary care billing runs on high volume and fine details: E/M level selection by medical decision making or time, preventive visits and Medicare annual wellness visits, chronic care management, transitional care management, vaccines and their administration codes, and in-office testing under CLIA. We focus on capturing the services primary care teams already provide — care management time, same-day preventive and problem visits with modifier `25`, and add-on codes such as `G2211` where appropriate.

## Pediatrics

Well-child visits with age-based preventive codes, vaccine product and administration billing, developmental and behavioral screenings, and newborn care. Pediatric billing has heavy Medicaid and CHIP involvement, which means state-specific rules we track closely.

## Urology

Office procedures such as cystoscopy, urodynamics and prostate biopsy; injections and drug billing; and surgical cases with bundling rules that overlap with [urogynecology](/urogynecology-billing). Practices with both urology and urogynecology providers benefit from one team that understands both.

## Questions to ask any billing company — including us

Whatever your specialty, these questions help you judge whether a billing partner is a good fit:

- **How much experience do you have with my specialty specifically?** Ask for the codes, payers and denial patterns they know best.
- **Who will actually work my account?** A named team you can reach is better than an anonymous queue.
- **What will I see each month?** Ask for a sample report. Look for denial reasons, A/R aging and collection rate — not just "claims submitted."
- **How do you handle denials?** Every denial should be worked and categorized, not just resubmitted.
- **What happens if we part ways?** You should keep your data, your system access and a clean hand-off of open A/R.
- **Will you sign a BAA?** This is a legal requirement for any billing company that handles patient information.

We'd rather you ask these questions and choose the right partner than sign with us and be disappointed. If your specialty isn't a strong fit for our team, we'll say so.
